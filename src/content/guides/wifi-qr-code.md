# How to Make a Wi-Fi QR Code

A Wi-Fi QR code lets guests join your network by scanning, with no need to read out a long password. Most modern phones will offer to connect as soon as the camera recognises the code.

## Why use a Wi-Fi QR code?

If you run a cafe, guest house, office, salon or even just a busy household, you probably get asked for the Wi-Fi password all day. Passwords can be long and full of capital letters, numbers and symbols, which makes them easy to mistype. A QR code solves this and also means you never have to say your password out loud.

## The Wi-Fi QR format

A Wi-Fi QR code contains text written in a standard format that phones understand:

`WIFI:T:WPA;S:YourNetworkName;P:YourPassword;;`

Here is what each part means:

- **T** is the security type. Use WPA for most home and business networks. Use WEP for very old networks, or nopass for an open network.
- **S** is the network name (SSID), spelled exactly as it appears, including capital letters.
- **P** is the password.
- The two semicolons at the end close the code.

Example for a network called CafeGuest with the password Welcome2026:

`WIFI:T:WPA;S:CafeGuest;P:Welcome2026;;`

If your network name or password contains special characters such as a semicolon, comma, colon or backslash, put a backslash in front of that character.

## How to generate it

1. Write your Wi-Fi text in the format above.
2. Paste it into the SnapQR input box.
3. Generate and download the code.
4. Print it or display it where guests can see it.

## How guests use it

On most iPhones and Android phones, guests open the camera, point it at the code, and tap the pop-up that offers to join the network. No app is required on recent versions of iOS and Android.

## Security tips

- Anyone who can see the code can join your network, so only display it where you are happy for people to connect.
- Use a separate guest network if your router supports it. This keeps your main devices and files apart from visitors.
- If you change your Wi-Fi password, the old QR code stops working. Generate and print a new one.
- Do not post a Wi-Fi code that includes your private password on public social media.

## Where to put it

Frame it on a table, stick it near the reception desk, or add it to a welcome card in a guest room. Add a small line such as "Scan to join our Wi-Fi" so the purpose is clear.

## Troubleshooting

- **Nothing happens when scanning:** Check that the text starts with WIFI: in capital letters and ends with two semicolons.
- **Phone says wrong password:** Re-type the password carefully, paying attention to capital letters and similar-looking characters such as O and 0.
- **Older phone will not scan:** Install any free QR scanner app, or enter the details manually.

A Wi-Fi QR code is a small touch that makes your space feel more welcoming and saves you from repeating yourself.
