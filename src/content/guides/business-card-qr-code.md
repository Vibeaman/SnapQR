# How to Add a QR Code to Your Business Card

A business card is small, and it can only hold so much information. A QR code fixes that. With one scan, someone can open your website, save your contact details, or message you on WhatsApp, with no typing at all. Here is how to do it properly.

## Why put a QR code on a business card?

People often lose cards or never get around to typing in the details. A QR code makes it easy to follow up. It also lets you share more than space allows: a portfolio, a booking page, a social media profile, or a full contact record. For freelancers, designers, developers and small business owners, it is a simple way to look more professional and make your card useful.

## Step 1: Decide what the code should do

Pick one main action. Too many options confuse people. Good choices include:

- **Your website or portfolio.** The safest and most flexible option.
- **A contact card (vCard).** Lets people save your name, phone and email straight to their phone.
- **A WhatsApp chat link.** Great for businesses that take orders or enquiries on WhatsApp.
- **A booking or order page.** Ideal if you sell services.

If you are unsure, link to a page on your own website. You can change that page whenever your details change, without reprinting your cards.

## Step 2: Generate the code

1. Open SnapQR.
2. Paste your link or text into the input box.
3. Choose your colors. Dark on light works best.
4. Set error correction to Medium or High.
5. Generate and download a high-quality image.

## Step 3: Get the size right

On a standard business card (about 9 cm by 5 cm), the QR code should be at least **2 cm by 2 cm**. Smaller than that and many phones struggle. Leave an empty border around the code so it can be read clearly.

## Step 4: Place it well

- Put the code on the back of the card if the front is crowded.
- Add a short label such as "Scan to save my contact" or "Scan to see my work". This tells people what will happen.
- Keep text, lines and images from touching the code.
- Avoid placing it near a fold or the cut edge of the card.

## Step 5: Test before you print

Print one sample at the real size and scan it with at least two different phones. Check it in normal room light. Only print a full batch when it scans quickly every time. Remember that matte paper scans more reliably than very glossy finishes.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- Making the code too small to scan.
- Using a very long link, which creates a dense, hard-to-read pattern. Use a short page address instead.
- Pointing the code at a page that is slow or broken on mobile.
- Forgetting a label, so people do not know why they should scan it.
- Using low contrast colors, such as light grey on white.

## Final thoughts

A QR code is one of the cheapest upgrades you can make to a business card. Choose one clear action, print it large enough, test it, and your card will do much more than list a phone number.
