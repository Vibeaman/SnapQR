# How to Use QR Codes to Market a Small Business

For a small business, marketing has to be affordable and easy to track. QR codes are cheap, simple to make, and connect your printed materials to your online presence. Used well, they help turn a passing customer into a follower, a lead or a sale.

## Why QR codes work for small businesses

- They cost almost nothing to create.
- They connect offline marketing to your online pages.
- They save customers from typing long addresses.
- They make printed materials interactive.
- They work for almost every type of business, from food sellers to salons to freelancers.

## Practical ways to use them

**1. Flyers and posters.** Add a code that links to your offer, your catalogue or your WhatsApp. A short line like "Scan to order" gives people a reason to act.

**2. Packaging and receipts.** Place a code on bags, boxes or receipts that links to a review page, your social media or a reorder link. This turns a one-time buyer into a repeat customer.

**3. Shop windows and counters.** Passers-by can scan to see opening hours, prices or your menu, even when you are closed.

**4. Product labels.** Link to usage instructions, ingredients or a short video that explains the product.

**5. Business cards.** Link to your website, portfolio or a saved contact card.

**6. Menus and price lists.** Let customers view a digital version that you can update at any time.

**7. Feedback and reviews.** Link to a short feedback form or review page. More reviews help new customers trust you.

## Step by step: running a simple QR campaign

1. **Choose one goal.** For example, more WhatsApp orders, more followers, or more reviews.
2. **Prepare the destination.** A clear, mobile-friendly page with one action.
3. **Create the code** with SnapQR and download it in high quality.
4. **Add a call to action** beside the code, such as "Scan for 10 percent off".
5. **Print at a good size** and place it where people already pause.
6. **Test** on several phones before printing in bulk.
7. **Measure results.** Count how many orders, messages or follows you get after the campaign.

## Tracking your results

A static code has no built-in scan counter, but you can still measure success. Use a separate landing page or a special offer code for each campaign, and see how many people use it. You can also ask new customers how they heard about you. If you use a website, check your visitor statistics for traffic coming from your printed materials.

## Mistakes to avoid

- Linking to a page that is not mobile friendly
- Printing the code too small
- Giving no reason to scan
- Using low contrast colors
- Placing the code somewhere people cannot reach or scan, such as high on a wall or behind glass glare
- Forgetting to test before printing

## Make it feel trustworthy

Add your business name and logo near the code so that customers know who it is from. This also helps protect against fake codes being placed over yours.

## Summary

QR codes are a low-cost way for small businesses to connect printed materials with online actions. Pick a clear goal, send people to a good mobile page, give them a reason to scan, and track what happens. Start with one simple campaign and build from there.
